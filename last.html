Local Anesthetic Systemic Toxicity — recognition and treatment.

Reference only. Summary for education and quick reference. Not a substitute for clinical judgment, current guidelines, or local protocols. Verify all doses and intervals before use.
Recognition
• CNS (early): perioral numbness, metallic taste, tinnitus, agitation, confusion, seizures.
• Cardiovascular: hypertension/tachycardia then bradycardia, hypotension, arrhythmia, asystole.
• Onset may be delayed — monitor after large-volume blocks.
Immediate management
1Stop injecting local anesthetic. Call for help.
2Manage airway — 100% oxygen, prevent hypoxia and acidosis.
3Control seizures — benzodiazepine (avoid large propofol doses if unstable).
4Lipid emulsion 20%: bolus 1.5 mL/kg over 1 min, then 0.25 mL/kg/min infusion.
5Repeat bolus 1–2× for persistent collapse; double infusion rate if BP remains low.
6If cardiac arrest: ACLS with reduced adrenaline doses (≤1 mcg/kg boluses); avoid vasopressin, Ca-channel/β-blockers, lidocaine.
Lipid emulsion — quick dose (70 kg)
StepDose
Initial bolus~105 mL over 1 min
Infusion~18 mL/min (0.25 mL/kg/min)
Maximum~12 mL/kg total
Important notes
• Lipid emulsion must be immediately available wherever LAs are used.
• Continue monitoring ≥4–6 h after cardiovascular events, ≥2 h after limited CNS events.
• Recovery may be prolonged — propofol is NOT a substitute for lipid emulsion.
