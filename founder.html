About the founder

Dr. Giga Nadiradze

Consultant anesthesiologist and intensive care physician
Founder of Anestheo

Anesthesia is the part of surgery patients understand least and worry about most. I have spent more than fifteen years on the other side of that gap, in operating rooms, recovery units and intensive care. Anestheo is my attempt to close it.

From Georgia to clinical medicine

I am from Georgia, and that is where medicine started for me. My medical education and my first years of training happened there, in the ordinary way these things happen: long hours, a great deal of watching, and the slow discovery that the quiet specialties are often the ones holding everything together.

Anesthesia was that discovery for me. It sits at the centre of surgical care and most patients never really see it. You meet someone for a few minutes, you take responsibility for them while they are at their most vulnerable, and if the work is done well they remember almost none of it.

Years at the bedside

Much of my professional life has been built in Israel, where I have now worked for more than nine years. A large part of that has been at Barzilai Medical Center, in anesthesia and intensive care.

The work spans the whole arc of a surgical patient: assessing them beforehand, caring for them in the operating room, seeing them through recovery, and looking after the ones who end up in intensive care. Across that time it has covered a broad range of surgical specialties and a great many critically ill patients.

What years of that teach you is not mainly technical. It is that preparation decides most of what happens later, that judgement matters more than protocol when the two disagree, and that the ability to explain something clearly, to a frightened person with very little time, is a clinical skill rather than a courtesy.

When medicine becomes more than routine

Some of the most important lessons in medicine are learned when the routine disappears. Working in anesthesia and intensive care through periods of war and major emergency pressure reinforced what matters when a department is stretched: preparation done in advance, decisions made clearly, and calm communication with patients and with the team.

Those periods did not change what I believe good care looks like. They made it much harder to pretend that anything else is good enough.

Teaching the next clinician

Alongside clinical work I have taught residents, medical students and paramedics, and taken part in clinical education, case discussions and practical workshops.

Teaching is where I first noticed the thing Anestheo is built on. Knowing something and being able to hand it to another person are two different abilities, and the second one is what makes the first useful. A case discussion that lands, a workshop where the room actually follows, an explanation that survives contact with a tired trainee at the end of a shift: that is the same problem as explaining anesthesia to a patient, in different clothes.

Why I built Anestheo

Patients meet anesthesia at one of the most stressful moments of their lives. They are told they will be asleep, handed a consent form, and introduced to the person responsible for them shortly before the operation begins.

On our side of it, the picture is complete. We know the monitors, the drugs, the risks that matter for this particular person and the decisions that will be made. From where the patient is sitting, there is an unfamiliar room and a process nobody has had time to explain. That distance is the problem Anestheo exists to reduce.

For patients, that means understanding what is actually going to happen and being able to prepare for it properly, so that less of the day is a surprise. For clinicians, it means focused tools that respect how fast and how crowded real clinical work is, built around the way the work is actually done rather than around how it looks from outside.

Anestheo does not diagnose anyone and it decides nothing. Your anesthesiologist does that, with you. What it changes is how much you already understand when that conversation starts.

Medicine is built on knowledge, but trust begins with understanding.

That idea is at the heart of Anestheo.
