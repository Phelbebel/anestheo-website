General / Other Surgery

Anesthesia for abdominal and general procedures.

Educational information only. This guide is general and may not reflect your specific situation. Always follow the instructions from your own surgical and anesthesia team.
1 · Anesthesia commonly used

General surgery — such as gallbladder removal, hernia repair, or appendix surgery — is most often done under general anesthesia.

Some procedures may use spinal anesthesia or a regional block, sometimes combined with general anesthesia for pain relief.

2 · How to prepare
Attend your pre-operative assessment.
Optimize chronic conditions such as diabetes and blood pressure.
Arrange help at home during early recovery.
Stop smoking as early as possible to improve healing.

4 · Medication guidance

Review all medications with your team, especially blood thinners and diabetes medication.

You'll receive specific instructions on what to take or hold on the morning of surgery.

5 · On the day of surgery
IV placed for fluids and medication.
General anesthesia keeps you fully asleep with a protected airway.
Keyhole (laparoscopic) surgery often means a faster recovery.
You're monitored closely throughout and in recovery.
6 · Common risks & side effects
Nausea and sore throat — common and treatable.
Shoulder-tip pain after laparoscopic surgery — from the gas used, settles quickly.
Blood clots — reduced with movement and prophylaxis.
Serious complications are uncommon.
7 · Recovery expectations

Recovery depends on the procedure and whether it was keyhole or open surgery.

Early walking, good pain control, and gradual return to eating support a smooth recovery. Many keyhole procedures are day cases or a short stay.

8 · Frequently asked questions
Will I be fully asleep?
Most general surgery uses general anesthesia, so yes.
What is keyhole surgery?
Minimally invasive surgery through small incisions — usually less pain and faster recovery.
Why does my shoulder hurt after?
Gas used during laparoscopic surgery can cause temporary shoulder-tip pain.
When can I eat?
Usually soon after, starting with fluids and light food as advised.
