# Design Principles

Eight beliefs. Every screen obeys all of them. They are not preferences — they
are the visible form of the Foundation. When two of them seem to conflict,
the earlier one wins.

---

## 1 · Quiet Authority

**Why.** Medicine earns trust by being certain without being loud. The Foundation
asks us to remove luck, not to perform confidence. A page that shouts is a page
that is unsure. Ours is the voice of someone who is the best at this and feels no
need to say so.

**In practice.** Calm surfaces. Few words. Generous space. Nothing blinks,
counts, or demands. Confidence is shown by what we are willing to leave out.

**Never.** Hype, urgency, exclamation, animation-for-attention, badges, banners.

## 2 · Editorial before interface

**Why.** Anestheo is a company before it is software. We compose pages the way a
serious publication composes a spread — type, rhythm, and space first; controls
second. This is also what keeps us timeless: editorial design has looked correct
for seventy years; interface trends date in eighteen months.

**In practice.** Think page, not screen. A left axis, a measured column, a
considered break. The interface appears only where it does real work.

**Never.** A layout that could only exist because it is a website.

## 3 · Typography creates hierarchy

**Why.** If type is set correctly, you need almost nothing else — no boxes,
rules, colors, or icons to tell the eye where to go. Typography is the cheapest,
most timeless, most honest way to build order.

**In practice.** Scale and weight do the work. One dominant line per view. See
`TYPOGRAPHY.md`.

**Never.** Color, borders, or shadow used to manufacture a hierarchy that the
type should have created.

## 4 · Restraint is confidence

**Why.** Anyone can add. Knowing what to withhold is the rarer discipline, and it
reads instantly as quality. The most expensive-feeling thing on a page is the
space we chose not to fill.

**In practice.** When a screen feels slightly too empty, it is probably right.
One accent color, used twice. One motion. Two typefaces.

**Never.** Decoration. Anything present only to look designed.

## 5 · Product is always the proof

**Why.** The Foundation makes a claim about better care. The product is the
evidence that the claim is real. We never hide it — and we never reduce it to an
abstraction. It is shown with dignity, because a company whose purpose is making
care visible would never conceal its own work.

**In practice.** Real screenshots, real decisions, shown whole and legible, lit
by the Point. See `PRODUCT_PRESENTATION.md`.

**Never.** Mockups, fake UI, browser frames, feature walls.

## 6 · Remove before adding

**Why.** Improvement usually means subtraction. Every element added must defend
its existence against the version of the page without it. Most cannot.

**In practice.** Before shipping, remove one thing. If the page is not worse, it
stays removed. Ask of everything: *what would Apple remove?*

**Never.** Adding a section because pages "usually have one."

## 7 · One idea per screen

**Why.** Attention is singular. A screen that says two things says neither. The
eye should always know the one place to land.

**In practice.** Each section carries a single statement, a single proof, or a
single product moment — never a competition between them.

**Never.** Two headlines, two heroes, or two calls to action sharing a viewport.

## 8 · One emotional beat per section

**Why.** A page is a sequence of feelings, not a list of facts. Tension, then
hope, then proof, then relief, then invitation. If a section has no beat, it is
not a section — it is filler, and filler dilutes the whole.

**In practice.** Name the beat before you design the section. If you cannot name
it, cut the section.

**Never.** A section that exists to inform without making the reader feel the
next step is inevitable.

---

These eight are the constitution of the surface. `FOUNDATION.md` is why we exist;
this is how that existence is allowed to look.
