# Product Presentation

How we show the work. This is where most companies betray themselves — with
glossy mockups of software that doesn't exist, or walls of features begging to be
noticed. We do the opposite, because the Foundation requires it: *Product is
always the proof* (Principle 5). The product is the evidence that the claim of
the Foundation is real. It is shown with dignity and restraint, and it is always
real.

---

## The first law: only real work

- **Real screenshots.** What we show is what exists, captured from the running
  product.
- **Real decisions.** The data on screen is a real clinical situation — a day's
  operating list, a patient's actual journey — not lorem-ipsum or a staged demo.
- **Real, whole, legible.** We show the product complete enough to be understood,
  not cropped into an abstraction. A visitor should grasp what we build, not
  admire a fragment. *One beautifully presented product is stronger than an
  artistic crop.*

If it does not exist yet, we do not show it. We would rather say nothing than
show a thing that isn't real. (When the AI assistant ships, it earns its image.
Until then, it lives in words, not in a fabricated panel.)

---

## Never

- **Browser mockups.** No window chrome, no traffic-light dots, no laptop frames.
  These say "marketing render," and they date instantly.
- **Fake UI.** No invented dashboards, no idealized screens we don't ship.
- **Feature walls.** No grid of a dozen product cards. A wall of features is a
  company unsure which one matters. Show one thing, fully.
- **Three competing screenshots in a row.** One product owns its moment.

---

## How the product is shown

The product lives in the **dark workspace** (`COLOR.md`) — the room where the
work happens — and it is presented as a single, considered object:

- **One product per moment**, given real size and a generous surround of dark.
  The space around it is what makes it feel like an object worth owning, not a
  screenshot pasted onto a page.
- **Frameless**, lifted from the dark by a 1px hairline and a single soft shadow.
  Nothing else.
- **Lit by the Point.** The dark frame declares itself `lit` (`POINT.md`); the
  work sits in the pool of the one steady light. This is what gives the product
  its soul — it is not presented under stage lights, it is *watched over*.
- **One caption.** A tracked uppercase label (the product's name) and one serif
  line — the **decision it improves**, never a feature (`COPY.md`):
  - `OPERATING LIST` — *Your day, decided before it begins.*
  - `THE PATIENT JOURNEY` — *Every patient arrives prepared — and never alone.*

The composition you would screenshot is a beautiful product. The Point is only
felt. That balance — product as hero, Point as soul — is the whole of how we
present our work.

---

## Why dignity, not spectacle

A company whose purpose is making care visible would never hide its product — and
would never cheapen it with theatrics. Spectacle (glows, gradients, exploded
views, animated reveals) says *look how impressive this looks.* Dignity says
*this is real, and it is enough.* Dignity is the more confident position, and
confidence is the brand.

The product does not need to be sold. It needs to be **seen** — clearly, calmly,
and in the steady light of someone who is paying attention.
