# Copy — the Anestheo voice

We write the way a great clinician speaks: plainly, certainly, and without a
trace of salesmanship. The words are part of the design. A single hyped sentence
undoes a page of restraint.

The voice descends from the Values — **rigor, evidence, humility** — and from the
Promise: *best judgment, not best guess.* We do not perform confidence; we state
what is true and stop.

---

## Five qualities

- **Plain.** Use the simplest true word. If a sentence needs decoding, rewrite it.
- **Certain.** Statements, not pitches. We end on a full stop, never a question
  mark and never an exclamation. A period is a decision; an exclamation is a plea.
- **Human.** Write to a frightened patient and a tired clinician — both at once.
  Warmth without sentimentality.
- **Exact.** Say the specific thing. "The same standard, in every hospital" beats
  "better outcomes everywhere."
- **Quiet.** Trust that the reader is intelligent. Never over-explain, and never
  explain the metaphor — let meaning be felt, then understood.

---

## Never use these words

They are banned because each one is a company telling you it is impressive
instead of being impressive:

> revolutionary · cutting-edge · AI-powered · seamless · innovative · world-class ·
> game-changing · empower · unlock · supercharge · next-generation · best-in-class

If a sentence leans on any of them, it has no real claim underneath. Find the
real claim and say it plainly.

---

## Prefer this register

Plain. Certain. Human. Exact. Quiet. Some calibrations:

- "AI-powered clinical assistant" → *"the reasoning of the field, at the bedside."*
- "Seamless patient onboarding" → *"every patient arrives prepared."*
- "Revolutionizing perioperative care" → *"nobody should have to be lucky to
  receive great care."*
- "Get started today!" → *"find your place in it."*

---

## Rules by kind

### Headlines

- A truth, not a tagline. The strongest headline is a line of the Foundation.
- Short — aim for six words, rarely more than ten.
- End in a full stop. It is a statement, not a question.
- One headline per screen (Principle 7). Never two competing.
- Set in the serif (`TYPOGRAPHY.md`). Let scale carry it; never bold it.

### Sub-lines and paragraphs

- One clarifying line, or none. We almost never write a paragraph; if we must, it
  is two or three short sentences.
- The sub orients or deepens — it never repeats the headline in smaller type.
- No hype, no adjectives doing the work a noun should do.

### Calls to action

- Calm verbs. *Find your path. See how it works. I deliver care.*
- Never urgency. No "now," "today," "free," "instant," exclamation marks.
- A CTA is an invitation, not a conversion. It assumes the reader will choose
  well, because the page already earned the choice.

### Product descriptions

- Name the **decision the product improves**, not the feature. The Mission is the
  lens: *does this help a perioperative team make a better decision?* Describe
  that.
  - "Operating List" → *"your day, decided before it begins."*
  - "Live Tools" → *"every calculation, certain — never a guess."*
- One line per product moment. Dignity, not a feature list (`PRODUCT_PRESENTATION.md`).

---

## Punctuation and tone

- **Full stops, not exclamation marks.** We are certain, not excited.
- **Em dashes** for the considered pause; sparingly.
- No ALL-CAPS for emphasis (tracked uppercase labels are a *typographic* device,
  not a shout — `TYPOGRAPHY.md`).
- No emoji in product or brand surfaces.

---

## The final test

Read the line aloud as if you were the anesthesiologist who has done this ten
thousand times, talking to one frightened person. If it sounds like marketing,
it is wrong. If it sounds like someone certain and kind, it is Anestheo.
