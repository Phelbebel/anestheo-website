Oncogynecology

Anesthesia for gynecologic oncology surgery.

Educational information only. This guide is general and may not reflect your specific situation. Always follow the instructions from your own surgical and anesthesia team.
1 · Anesthesia commonly used

Gynecologic cancer surgery is usually performed under general anesthesia, sometimes combined with an epidural or spinal for pain relief during and after surgery.

These can be longer operations, so the anesthesia team focuses on comfort, pain control, and a smooth recovery.

2 · How to prepare
Attend a pre-assessment to optimize your health before surgery.
Discuss pain-relief options including epidural analgesia.
Arrange support at home for a longer recovery.
Ask about prehabilitation — improving fitness and nutrition beforehand.

4 · Medication guidance

Review all medications with your team, including blood thinners and any cancer treatments.

Some medications are continued, others adjusted — you'll get specific instructions.

5 · On the day of surgery
IV placed; an epidural may be offered for pain control.
General anesthesia keeps you fully asleep.
Warming, fluids, and clot prevention are used for longer surgery.
You're monitored closely throughout and afterward.
6 · Common risks & side effects
Nausea — actively prevented and treated.
Blood clots — reduced with prophylaxis and early movement.
Sore throat from the breathing tube.
Your team manages pain proactively, often with an epidural or PCA pump.
7 · Recovery expectations

An epidural or patient-controlled analgesia (PCA) can give excellent pain relief in the first days.

Recovery depends on the extent of surgery; early mobilization, good nutrition, and breathing exercises speed healing.

8 · Frequently asked questions
Will I have an epidural?
It's often offered for these procedures because it provides excellent pain relief.
How is pain managed?
With a combination — epidural or PCA pump plus regular medication.
How long will I stay?
It depends on the surgery; your team will give you an estimate.
What is prehabilitation?
Improving fitness and nutrition before surgery to recover better afterward.
